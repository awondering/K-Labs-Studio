Recognising thread tunnels beside guide feet

In brief

A tunnel is an opening under turns bridging a guide-foot edge. Locate it in the dry wrap so it is not confused with a finish bubble.

Inspect the edge of the foot

Look at the guide foot from the side. Where the turns bridge the transition from foot to blank, you may see a space underneath. Flex Coat identifies an air pocket at the foot, blank and thread junction. Describe a visible gap in the dry wrap separately from any bubble seen after coating.

Compare a dry wrap on the same profile

Use a sample with a similar frame shape and watch where the thread first climbs the edge. Note whether the turns stay next to one another through that part of the profile. The spool and feed settings belong in the sample notes.

Keep the foot location in the report

If the opening is repeated on several guides, identify whether they are the same frame or whether it only occurs on one style of foot. Use that comparison to discuss the wrap with the component maker rather than choosing a remedy solely from the word tunnel.
