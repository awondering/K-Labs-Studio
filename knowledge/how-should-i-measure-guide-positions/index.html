How to measure guide positions and spacing

In brief

Measure every guide from the same end of the rod to the same point on each guide. Spacing is then the difference between neighbouring positions.

Position and spacing

A guide's position is its distance from one fixed end of the rod. The spacing between two guides is the difference between their positions. For guides at 110, 235 and 380 mm from the tip:

  • Tip to guide 1: 110 mm
  • Guide 1 to guide 2: 235 − 110 = 125 mm
  • Guide 2 to guide 3: 380 − 235 = 145 mm

Use the same point on every guide

Measure to the same feature on every guide, such as the ring centre. If one guide is measured to its foot instead, both spacings next to it are wrong by the distance from foot to ring centre: one too long, the other too short.

From the tip or from the butt

The two are linked by the rod's length: distance from the butt = rod length − distance from the tip. On a 2100 mm rod, a guide 380 mm from the tip is 1720 mm from the butt. Spacings come out the same either way, but a list that mixes the two does not add up.

Comparing two layouts

Before comparing guide tables from different sources, check that both measure from the same end, to the same point on each guide, in the same unit. Otherwise one rod can look like two different layouts.
