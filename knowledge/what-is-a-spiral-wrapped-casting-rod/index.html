What a spiral-wrapped casting rod is

In brief

The reel stays above the blank while the line path moves around it and continues through guides underneath toward the tip.

Follow the line from reel to tip

Start with the rod held reel up. Near the handle the line leaves the reel above the blank, then moves around the side and reaches runners underneath. The name describes the route of the line and guide orientations, rather than the pattern of thread around a guide foot.

Recognise two layout styles

A gradual spiral may use several rotated guides between top and bottom. In Kirkman’s Simple Spiral, the entry guide remains on top and most of the train lies underneath, with a side guide available for clearance. Both look different from an inline guides-on-top casting layout.

Read each guide’s two coordinates

On a spiral drawing, look for where each guide sits along the rod and how it is rotated around the blank. A guide can occupy the same station as on an inline rod and still point in a different direction. Keep position and rotation separate when reading or describing the build.
