What causes fisheyes in rod wrap finish

In brief

A fisheye is a spot where the coating pulls away from the surface. Investigate the local surface and the tools that touched the mix.

Look for retreating coating

A fisheye leaves a small area with reduced coverage as the wet coating draws away. An air bubble can also leave a round-looking mark. Recording what the coating did while wet makes the two observations easier to distinguish.

Trace what touched the affected area

Flex Coat identifies silicone contamination as a cause for its finish. Record whether the defect is confined to a spot, repeated on several wraps or appears on a test sample from the same cup. Review which tools, surfaces or handling steps those areas shared.

Check the finish maker before rework

Use the product’s surface and equipment guidance to plan the next sample. Compare results before deciding whether the same problem is likely on the rod. A clean-looking surface alone is not evidence of compatibility with another cleaner or coating.
