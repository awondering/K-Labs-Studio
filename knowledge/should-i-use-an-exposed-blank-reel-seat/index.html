Choosing an exposed-blank reel seat

In brief

Compare an exposed-blank seat with a conventional barrel by dry fitting the actual reel and checking hand contact and blank fit.

Try both hand positions

An exposed seat has an opening that leaves part of the blank accessible. Put the intended reel in each candidate seat. Check where the angler’s fingers land and whether they actually reach the window in their normal grip.

Measure the fit at the chosen station

Check how the chosen seat is sized and compare the blank at the planned position. A fitted model and an arbor-supported barrel use different fit arrangements. An exposed window is a design choice, while the manufacturer’s fit dimensions are a separate requirement.

Include the adjoining grips

Mock up the rear grip and any foregrip with the seat. If the rear grip or hood shape moves the hand away from the window, try the conventional seat in the same handle position before ordering.
