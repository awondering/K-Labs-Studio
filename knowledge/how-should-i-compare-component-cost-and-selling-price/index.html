Margin and markup: what is the difference?

In brief

Markup is profit as a share of the cost; margin is profit as a share of the selling price. The same sale gives two different percentages.

The formulas

  • Profit = selling price − cost
  • Markup = profit ÷ cost × 100
  • Margin = profit ÷ selling price × 100

Worked example

A component bought for $40 and sold for $60 makes $20 profit. That is a 50% markup but a 33.3% margin.

Converting between them

  • Margin = markup ÷ (100 + markup) × 100
  • Markup = margin ÷ (100 − margin) × 100

A 50% markup is a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin needs a 100% markup.

Pricing for a target margin

Selling price = cost ÷ (1 − margin ÷ 100). For a 40% margin on a $40 cost: $40 ÷ 0.6 = $66.67.

Leave tax out of both figures

Compare like with like. If the selling price includes a sales tax such as GST, take the tax out before working out margin or markup: at a 15% rate, a $69.00 tax-inclusive price is $60.00 before tax.
