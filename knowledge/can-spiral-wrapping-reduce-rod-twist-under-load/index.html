How spiral wrapping changes rod twist

In brief

Moving the working guides beneath a casting rod changes the leverage of line force around the blank and can reduce its tendency to roll.

Look at the force across the blank

Look along the blank from the butt. In that end-on view, the line’s pull on a guide twists the blank if the projected line of action misses the centre of the blank. If the projected force points through the centre, it produces no twisting moment about that axis. Moving the guide around the blank can change the size or direction of the moment.

Compare the upper and lower positions

With the working guides above the blank, a sideways displacement can let the line pull the rod farther from its upright position. An underside running train is closer to alignment with a downward pull in the usual fishing orientation. That is the mechanical reason to consider a spiral, rather than an assumption about blank spine.

Account for the whole layout

The result depends on where the force acts, the direction of pull and the guide geometry. The first guide and any side guides still contribute their own forces. A spiral layout does not make every possible line pull pass through the blank axis.
