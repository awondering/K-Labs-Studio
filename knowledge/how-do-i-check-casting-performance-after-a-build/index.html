Checking casting performance on a finished rod

In brief

Compare repeat casts with the intended outfit and keep a short record of distance, control and any recurring line problems.

Define what counts as a good cast

Choose a task the customer will actually use, such as placing a small lure near a target rather than throwing as far as possible. Pick the intended reel, line, leader and a lure within the rod’s specified range. Record those along with wind direction and the reference rod, if you use one.

Keep the comparison repeatable

For each setup, make a series of comparable casts in the same direction. Keep the caster, target and rig consistent where possible. Record where the casts land and note how often the knot catches, line hits the blank or the spool overruns. One unusually long cast tells you little about a recurring problem.

Report the conditions with the result

Say which conditions you actually checked. For example, a rod may place repeated casts accurately with a short leader while the customer’s longer leader brings the knot into the running train. Record that as a different setup to assess rather than treating it as covered by the first result.
