Single-foot and double-foot rod guides

In brief

Compare the number of attachment feet, the wrap footprint, and the frame’s intended job before substituting guides.

Count the feet on the component

A single-foot frame meets the blank at one foot. A double-foot frame has feet on both sides of the ring. That gives you one or two places to wrap respectively. In the Fuji K-Series, KW is an example of a double-foot frame and KL is an example of a single-foot frame.

Match the job as well as the size

On a replacement, note whether the old guide was a stripper, reducer or runner. Compare the new frame’s ring height, clear opening and feet with the original at that station. Foot count describes how the frame mounts. Check the guide family and line path to decide which station it suits.

Lay the frames next to the rod

If a replacement has feet that extend beyond the old wrap marks, record the new footprint before you agree on the appearance of the repair. For a new build, compare the actual footprint and mass of the candidate frames instead of treating foot count as a weight specification.
