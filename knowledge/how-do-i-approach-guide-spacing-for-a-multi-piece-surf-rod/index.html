Laying out guides on a multi-piece surf rod

In brief

Plot the actual joint positions and reel reference before adapting a surf guide chart to the assembled blank.

Mark the joints on the same drawing

Lay out the rod in its assembled configuration and mark where each section meets the next. Use one distance reference for the joint marks and guide stations. St. Croix’s Seage model table illustrates why the model matters: rods with the same overall length and piece count can have different section splits.

Overlay a matching starting layout

Choose a guide reference for the reel type and rod family, then draw the guide feet as well as the ring positions near each joint. A ring centre can appear clear on a chart while the footprint of your selected frame reaches the joint area. Check where the pieces separate before keeping that station.

Keep track of the neighbouring stations

If a frame and joint compete for space, identify the affected guide and review its neighbouring gaps and ring positions as a group. Record the position you actually settled on against the assembled blank. That makes the chart usable when a section is replaced or a guide is reordered.
